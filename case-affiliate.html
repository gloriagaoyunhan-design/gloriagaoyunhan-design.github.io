The challenge

Partners couldn't grow their affiliate networks without relying on the Operations team

Our platform lets partners work with affiliates who refer new clients and earn a negotiated share of revenue. Previously, adding affiliates, agreeing splits, and reviewing performance required manual setup through an account manager, which took days and created heavy ops workload. This slowed partner growth and buried ops in repetitive setup requests.

Business Goals

Give partners full control of their network

Enable self-service across the full lifecycle from adding an affiliate to performance monitoring.

Reduce operation cost and manual effort

Remove manual relationship setup and shorten the time for a new affiliate to start earning.

Preserve partner-affiliate transparency and privacy balance

Keep the existing balance between what partners need to manage the relationship and what affiliates should (and shouldn't) see.

Process

Designing without direct access to partners and affiliates

We could not interview partners directly, so I partnered with sales and ops teammates who work with partners daily.

Understand the relationship, not just the feature

Before going to the drawing board, I started by understanding how partner-affiliate relationships work in real life: why partners add affiliates, how revenue splits are negotiated, and how the relationship evolves over time.

Map the end-to-end journey

I mapped the full journey for both parties, from building a network to monitoring performance. This helped us define scope early and surface edge cases before implementation.

Prioritize with PM using real usage patterns

We prioritized features based on frequency and impact. The goal was to keep a feature-dense surface scannable and decision-oriented.

AI-native approach to design delivery

Alongside the design work itself, this project doubled as an experiment in AI-native delivery workflow. We used Claude Design as the living source-of-truth and handover artefact, instead of relying only on static Figma files.

Throughout this experiment, we explored the strengths and limitations of this tool in a multi-stakeholder collaborative process.

Design Principles

  1. Overview first, detail on demand

    Give partners a quick overview first, then reveal depth when they choose to investigate.

  2. Put answers where users hesitate

    Surface key information at the moment of decision, rather than dumping everything upfront.

  3. Design for two audiences that don't fully trust each other

    Use permissions and disclosure patterns so each side can act confidently without having to worry about oversharing.

  4. Mobile parity

    ~19% of partners primarily use mobile, so mobile cannot be a reduced experience.

Solution

One page, two views of the same network

Two tabs present the same data differently, allowing users to compare performance and manage relationships quickly without excessive filtering.

Detail on demand via drawer

A detail drawer lets users review a program or relationship without losing context, keeping the main page fast to scan.

Guided setup for basic and advanced users

A linear flow supports the common setup path, with optional branches for advanced needs such as adding a second affiliate or configuring split-override rules by symbol and asset.

Full mobile experience

The mobile experience supports the same core workflows so partners can manage the network on the go.

Outcome

A Fully Self-Service Affiliate Management Platform

0 Manual ops tickets needed to add an affiliate, set a split, or check earnings
1 report Replacing a scattered process of emails, spreadsheets, and Jira tickets
2 groups Partner and affiliate each get controlled transparency on their relationship

What We'll Watch After Go-Live

The product is still in development. After launch, these metrics will show whether self-service is working:

Ops ticket volume

The direct measure of back-office dependency — it should trend towards zero for anything the new flow covers.

Time from add-affiliate to first active client

Indicator of whether partner network growth is following the easier network enablement.

Add-affiliate flow completion rate

Indicator of potential friction in the journey.
